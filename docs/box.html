What each part is

Arduino Nano

The brain. It runs your code.

Good to know: Plugs into the base board. Leave it on the base board all the time.

Nano base board

Gives every Nano pin three sockets in a row: S (signal), V (5 volts), G (ground).

Good to know: Most modules plug in with three jumpers, one in each. If it has a 3.3V/5V jumper, set it to 5V.

USB cable

Brings power to the Nano and carries your code across.

Good to know: If the computer cannot see the Nano, try another cable. Some only charge.

LEDs, assorted colours (25)

Small lights.

Good to know: They work one way round only: the long leg is plus (+). They need a 220 Ω resistor.

RGB LEDs (5)

Red, green and blue LEDs in one. Mix them to get any colour.

Good to know: Four legs. The longest is the shared one (ground, usually). Each colour leg needs a 220 Ω resistor.

IR sensor module

Sees things in front of it using invisible infrared light.

Good to know: Pins: OUT, VCC, GND. Output goes LOW when it sees something. A tiny screw sets the range.

Light sensor module (LDR)

Gives a number that changes with the light in the room.

Good to know: Use the AO pin for numbers. Pins: AO, VCC, GND (some modules also have DO).

Buzzer

Beeps.

Good to know: Two legs, the longer one is plus (+). Project 5 tells you if it can play notes (passive) or only beep (active).

Touch sensor module

A pad that feels a finger. Like a button with no moving part.

Good to know: Pins: I/O, VCC, GND. Output goes HIGH while you touch it.

Raindrop sensor

A sensing pad on a cable and a small module. Water on the pad lowers the number.

Good to know: Module pins: AO, DO, GND, VCC. Keep the module dry, only the pad gets wet.

Water level sensor

A strip with copper lines. The more of it is underwater, the bigger the number.

Good to know: Pins: S, +, -. Dip only the lines and dry it after. Water slowly eats the copper.

Ultrasonic sensor (HC-SR04)

Measures distance using sound you cannot hear.

Good to know: Four pins: VCC, Trig, Echo, GND. Two "eyes" on the front: one sends, one listens.

400-point breadboard

A board for building circuits without soldering.

Good to know: The five holes in one short row are joined. The long strips on the sides are power rails.

Jumper wires (3 packs of 40)

Wires with a plug on each end.

Good to know: Female end has a hole, male end has a pin. You should have female-female, male-female and male-male.

Not in the box: resistors. A resistor limits the current. LEDs need one. Buy a small pack of 220 Ω resistors (any value from 150 to 470 Ω works) at any electronics shop. The colours on a 220 Ω are red, red, brown. Projects 2 and 10 need them. Projects 3, 4 and 15 work without them by using the light on the Nano. Where to buy.

How the base board works

Each pin on the Nano has three sockets on the base board in a row.

How the breadboard works

  • The five holes in one short column (or row, depending how you turn it) are joined by metal under the plastic. Push two legs into the same group and they are connected.
  • The long strips along the sides are the power rails. We use one for ground. A jumper from any G pin to the rail gives every part on the board a ground.
  • The gap in the middle splits the board in two halves. The halves are not joined.

Jumper wires

  • Female-female (a hole at both ends): from a module pin to the base board pin. These go on most modules.
  • Male-female (a pin at one end, a hole at the other): the hole goes on the base board, the pin goes into the breadboard.
  • Male-male (a pin at both ends): inside the breadboard, or from rail to row.

If you are not sure which pack is which, look at the plug ends.
